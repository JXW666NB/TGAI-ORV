docs: run the reviewer's PyTorch reproduction and record what ORV has to match

The package runs cleanly on the V100. Full v2.1e, 434,229,976 parameters, fp16 autocast plus per-layer
checkpointing: 30 steps at B=8 and S=2048 gives 5,667 tok/s with a 10.28 GB peak, loss falling 10.76 to 3.89;
B=2 gives 3,494 tok/s at 8.59 GB. The smoke configuration finishes in 2.2 s at 37,964 tok/s. Their synthetic
corpus generates without trouble, so this is now a usable reference rather than an estimate.

Placed next to it, ORV's own figure is sobering: a six-layer simplified dense model at B=2 reaches a 10.80 GiB
backward peak, more than torch spends on twenty layers at B=8. The difference is structural, not tuning.

Reading the config corrects one of the reviewer's own priorities. The plan asks for sdpa_lowmem to grow from
64 to 128, but att_dhead is 128 for two layers and 96 for the other eighteen -- head dimension is never 64.
Extending to 128 alone leaves eighteen layers still materialising attention. The fix has to cover 96 as well,
or be written for arbitrary head dimension.

The dominant cause is that torch's autocast stores activations in fp16 while ORV's AMP casts only GEMM inputs
and keeps activations fp32, roughly doubling that term. That work is already listed as pending in
MULTIVENDOR_ROADMAP section 6 under real AMP with bf16 activation storage, and it has never been done. It is
the only lever that closes the gap; chunked cross-entropy and a wider lowmem kernel are secondary.

The document also notes that ORV has no port of this model yet, so no comparable number exists. Porting it --
RoPE, learnable RMSNorm, LayerScale, per-layer head dimensions and six MoE layers -- is the next step, and it
doubles as a test of whether ORV's layer set can express a real user's architecture.
