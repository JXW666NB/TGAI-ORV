docs: ablate fp16 and checkpointing on the reference model, and withdraw my own priority

The reviewer's reproduction supports --no-fp16 and --no-ckpt, so I measured what each actually buys on the V100.
The answer contradicts what I said last turn.

At B=8 and S=2048 with checkpointing, fp16 uses 10.28 GB against 12.61 GB for fp32 -- a ratio of 1.23, not the
2x I claimed. At B=2 it is 8.59 against 8.91, barely 1.04. Turning checkpointing off instead takes 10.28 to
28.24 GB, a factor of 2.75. So checkpointing is the dominant memory mechanism and ORV already has it, while the
activation dtype accounts for at most a fifth.

The reason is straightforward once seen: with checkpointing on, activations are already small, and the bulk is
parameters plus gradients plus optimizer state, 434M parameters at four bytes each and four copies, about
6.9 GB, which fp16 does not reduce at all.

Speed is the opposite story. The same ablations give 5,667 against 2,612 tok/s at B=8, so fp16 buys 2.17x there,
because it is tensor cores doing the work rather than activation storage. ORV's existing fp16, which casts only
GEMM inputs, measured 1.11x earlier.

So the document now ranks real AMP as a speed lever, not a memory lever, and the first priority becomes
understanding why ORV holds about 1.8 GiB of transient per layer during backward when the reference model's
whole-system figure works out near 0.17 GB per layer -- roughly ten times apart. That is where the 16 GB
failure lives, and it is a question about how many intermediates are alive at once rather than about precision.

Loss values are identical between the fp16 and fp32 runs on both batches, which is a useful sanity check that
the ablation changed only the arithmetic pathway.

Documentation only. Version stays 0.9.52.
