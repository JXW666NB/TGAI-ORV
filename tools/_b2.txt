docs: correct my own conclusion -- checkpointing is fine, the backward peak is the real cost

I wrote and published that checkpointing does not free activations. That was wrong, and the measurement that
produced it was flawed in the way I keep repeating: I read pool_stats peak_live_bytes, which is a global
high-water mark that never resets, while running L=2, 4 and 8 in one process with freshly allocated weights
each time. The apparent linear growth with depth was the accumulated weights of earlier iterations, not
activations.

Measured with current live bytes instead, checkpointing behaves correctly. At D=1280, dh=128, FF=5120,
S=2048, B=2 the live figure grows 0.035, 0.055, 0.074, 0.094, 0.113, 0.133 GiB across six layers, which is
19.6 MB a layer against a residual stream of 21 MB, with exactly one extra Tensor and one extra GpuBuf per
layer and no change after gc.collect(). One layer of activations is present at a time, as intended.

The real cost is the backward pass. For those same six layers the weights total 0.63 GiB, the gradients
settle at 0.759 GiB, and the backward peak reaches 10.799 GiB, roughly 1.8 GiB per layer. It falls back to
0.76 GiB afterwards, so this is transient rather than a leak. That figure reconciles both observed failures:
the P100 reviewer's 15.15 GiB at 434M parameters and 20 layers, and my own 31.29 GiB at 606M.

So checkpointing guarantees that only one layer's activations exist at a time, but cannot make that one
layer cheap. Hand-computing the layer at this shape gives about 1.2 to 1.4 GiB, dominated by the materialised
[BH, S, S] attention at 335 MB, which exists only because dh is 128 and sdpa_lowmem requires exactly 64. The
measured 1.8 GiB is the same order, so the opportunity is per-layer cost rather than a hidden retention bug.

Two directions follow: make sdpa_lowmem accept dh=128, and check whether the attention probability tensor and
its gradient can share storage. Before either, the next measurement must run each depth in a separate process
so the peak is clean, which decides whether the peak is constant per layer or accumulates across them.

The document is renamed from CHECKPOINT_BROKEN to MEMORY_BACKWARD_PEAK because the old name asserted the
wrong thing, and it opens with the correction and its cause.
