docs: checkpointing measurably does not free activations, which is why the P100 cannot train

I tried to show that 0.9.51 had already made the P100 configuration fit. It had not, and the measurement says
so plainly. Holding D=1280, dh=128, FF=5120, S=2048 and B=1 while varying only the layer count, the forward
peak with checkpointing grows 2.09, 3.83, 7.30 GiB for 2, 4 and 8 layers. Linear growth of roughly 0.87 GiB
per layer, and higher than the same model without checkpointing at 1.56, 3.09, 6.17. If checkpointing worked
the peak would be nearly independent of depth, differing only by the residual stream at about 21 MB a layer.

Reproducing the reviewer's configuration on the V100 also fails, running out at 31.29 GiB live -- my parameter
count was larger, 606M against their 434M, but both figures sit far above the roughly 7.4 GiB that a working
implementation should need: 6.4 GiB of parameters, gradients and AdamW state, plus about 1 GiB for a single
layer of activations.

So the 0.9.51 change, clearing _parents on the checkpoint output and dropping the clone, was insufficient. It
looked like a 30% win on the small dense test because at d=512 and S=512 each layer's activations are a few
megabytes; the defect only shows at S=2048 with dh=128, where one layer costs 0.87 GiB.

The document records four candidate causes in order of likelihood, led by the possibility that no_grad here
only suppresses _backward while operators still write _parents unconditionally, so clearing the output's
parents does not break whatever chain keeps the intermediates alive. It also records what a fixed version must
satisfy: peak differences under 0.2 GiB across depths, strictly below the no-checkpoint baseline, bit-identical
numerics, and the reviewer's own configuration completing a step on 16 GB.

For the P100 owner right now, the honest answer is that the current release cannot run that shape, and the
workaround is about eight layers at S=2048 or sixteen at S=1024 with batch one, roughly 13.4 GiB estimated and
still needing measurement. That is bypassing the problem, not solving it.
