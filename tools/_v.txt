docs: record the measured hardware topology, because two planning errors came from assuming it

I said the AMD Radeon integrated GPU and the V100 could form a mixed-vendor pair for TG Link. They cannot:
they are on different machines, and peer-to-peer requires one PCI bus. The topology is the reverse of what I
assumed -- the laptop running the agent has only the AMD iGPU with no NVIDIA at all, and the remote host
reached over SSH has both the V100 and a GT 730.

Probing both machines settles what is testable. The laptop has exactly one Vulkan device, the AMD iGPU at
Vulkan 1.3.260. The remote has exactly one CUDA device, the V100, and Vulkan enumerates nothing there:
vulkaninfo fails with "Failed to detect any valid GPUs" because HKLM\SOFTWARE\Khronos\Vulkan\Drivers does
not exist, so the loader is installed but no ICD is registered. The GT 730 is unusable through either API --
nvidia-smi does not list it, its driver is from 2020 against the V100's 2025 one, and Vulkan cannot initialise
on that machine at all.

So no machine has both CUDA and Vulkan working, no machine has two usable GPUs, and no mixed-vendor pair
exists anywhere in reach. That means TG Link's central hypothesis is blocked on hardware rather than on code,
and this needs to be stated plainly before anyone schedules work against it. It also means the Vulkan
forward and backward that VULKAN_BACKEND.md records were measured on the laptop, not on the V100 host.

What remains feasible is the static half of a probe, which enumerates capabilities on both machines, and
tier-3 host-staged collectives, which need several processes but no special hardware. Both P2P tiers need at
least two Vulkan devices on one machine; renting two cards on a single host would validate the mechanism even
if the vendors match.

Documented with the exact evidence -- driver versions, the missing registry key, the failing vulkaninfo line --
plus a note that the GT 730 might be recovered by registering nvoglv64.dll from the driver store, unverified.
INDEX and the skill now point at the inventory before any multi-GPU planning.
