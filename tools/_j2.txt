docs: write down what surpassing PyTorch would mean, measured against the reference we now have

The reference is concrete now, so the document states a single benchmark that any claim has to beat: the
reviewer's full v2.1e at B=8 and S=2048 on the V100, 5,667 tok/s at a 10.28 GB peak.

The honest current score is one win and one loss. ORV is faster per step, 2.58x on dense fp32 and 1.68x on MoE
fp32, because its kernels fuse work PyTorch leaves as separate launches, and it fits models PyTorch cannot
start, 3B MoE at 24.78 GiB where pure PyTorch runs out at 1.997B. It is also fatter, 1.2 to 1.9 times the
memory, and its fp16 buys 1.11x against PyTorch's 2.17x. Checkpointing only just reached parity in 0.9.53.

Everything that could exceed PyTorch comes from one structural fact: ORV owns the whole stack, so it can fuse
across operator boundaries instead of streaming every intermediate through HBM. An MLP chain of gate, up, silu
and mul writes and rereads three [B*S, FF] tensors that are 84 MB each at this shape, roughly 500 MB of traffic
per layer that fusion removes.

The other levers are memory, where selective checkpointing per layer and offloading optimizer state can push
under the 10.28 GB the reference spends; CUDA graphs by default, where the measured 627 ns dispatch is only 7%
of a large step but dominant for RL rollouts and small models; per-generation tuning tables, which pairs with
the plan to cover every Nvidia architecture; and verifiable numerics, which I think is the most distinctive
because PyTorch does not offer it. ORV already gates fp16 by shape with recorded error, and already has
deterministic reductions that reproduce bit for bit. Turning those into a promise -- an error bound plus
automatic fallback, and bit-exact reproducibility by default -- is a quality advantage rather than a speed one,
and nobody else is doing it.

The document also lists what not to attempt: chasing full API coverage, competing with torch.compile on how much
it can fuse automatically, claiming memory savings before they exist, or meeting NVLink-class setups with
host-staged multi-GPU.

Documentation only. Version stays 0.9.53.
